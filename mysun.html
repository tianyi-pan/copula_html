SUN(2,2) Parameterization

This note uses the SUN(2,2) correlation-scale parameterization from admvn::make_sun22_params: a length-10 free parameter vector mapping to the sn dp list (xi, Omega, Delta, tau=0, Gamma). The focus is a bivariate working sample and product-Gaussian EMS estimates of its dependence.

A general SUN is \(Y = U \mid V > 0\) with

\[ \begin{bmatrix} U \\ V \end{bmatrix} \sim \mathcal{N}\left( \begin{bmatrix} \xi \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} \Omega & \Sigma_{uv} \\ \Sigma_{uv}^\top & \Sigma_{vv} \end{bmatrix} \right), \]

where \(U,V\in\mathbb{R}^2\) and \(\xi=(\xi_1,\xi_2)^\top\).

Free parameters (length 10)

Location. \(\xi_1,\xi_2\).

Observed-scale covariance. Marginal standard deviations \(\nu_j>0\) (\(\Omega_{jj}=\nu_j^2\)) and free correlation coordinate \(\omega_{12}\) for the latent correlation \(C_u\):

\[ \Omega = D_u C_u D_u, \qquad D_u=\operatorname{diag}(\nu). \]

Skew loadings \(L\). All four entries free on the correlation scale:

\[ L = \begin{bmatrix} L_{11} & L_{12} \\ L_{21} & L_{22} \end{bmatrix}. \]

(\(L=0\) recovers a Gaussian; small diagonal \(L\) is a mild skew start, as in admvn::make_sun22_start_from_normal.)

Residual correlation. Free \(\gamma_{12}\) parameterizes the residual correlation of \(V\), so that \(\Sigma_{vv}=M+R\) with \(M=L C_u L^\top\) and unit diagonal.

sn quantities. Writing \(\Sigma_{uv}=D_u C_u L^\top\),

\[ \Delta = C_u L^\top, \qquad \Gamma = \Sigma_{vv}, \qquad \tau=0. \]

Valid parameters need \(\operatorname{diag}(L C_u L^\top)<1\).

Parameter order (same names as admvn):

xi1, xi2,
nu1, nu2,
omega12,
L11, L12, L21, L22,
gamma12

Box constraints. Package defaults from admvn::sun22_bounds(): diagonals of \(L\) in \([-1,1]\), off-diagonals in \([-0.5,0.5]\), \(\gamma_{12}\in[-5,5]\), \(\nu_j\ge 10^{-6}\).

Main analysis

All analyses below use Gaussian kernels for the marginals and product Gaussian kernels for the bivariate fits. The product kernel is

\[ K_{h_1,h_2}(t_1,t_2)=\frac{\phi(t_1/h_1)\phi(t_2/h_2)}{h_1h_2}, \]

where \(\phi\) is the standard normal density and \(h_1,h_2\) are kernel standard deviations. Every fit, including each cross-validation fold, uses maxit = 200. A fit that reaches this limit without convergence emits a warning.

Marginal density estimation

We use the notation of Braun and Stafford (2016), Section 3.1. Their EMS method uses local-constant kernel smoothing; the current degree = 2 implementation replaces that step with local quadratic regression of density heights.

Partition and EM step. Let \(R_i\) be observation \(i\)’s censoring interval, \(i=1,\ldots,n\). All interval endpoints together define cells \(Q_j\), \(j=1,\ldots,J\), with lengths \(v_j=\lVert Q_j\rVert\) and centres \(c_j\). Write \(p_j=\int_{Q_j}f(x)\,dx\) and \(\mathcal I_{ij}=\mathbf1\{Q_j\subseteq R_i\}\). Following equation (4), the iteration uses the piecewise-constant density

\[ \bar f^{(r)}(x)=\frac{\widehat p_j^{(r)}}{v_j},\quad x\in Q_j, \qquad \widehat p_j^{(0)}=\frac{v_j}{\sum_m v_m}. \]

The EM step reallocates each observation among compatible cells:

\[ M_j^{(r)}:=[\mathcal M(\widehat{\mathbf p}^{(r)})]_j =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{\widehat p_j^{(r)}\mathcal I_{ij}} {\sum_m\widehat p_m^{(r)}\mathcal I_{im}}. \]

Where the quadratic enters.

Braun and Stafford’s equation (5) updates the row vector by \[ \widehat{\mathbf p}^{(r+1)}=\mathcal M(\widehat{\mathbf p}^{(r)})\mathcal K_h, \] where \(\mathcal K_h\) is their kernel smoothing matrix.

Our code instead converts the EM masses to density heights \(q_j^{(r)}=M_j^{(r)}/v_j\). At each target centre \(c_k\), it fits a separate quadratic using \(t_{jk}=(c_j-c_k)/h\):

\[ \widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}^{(r)}(c_k) =\arg\min_{\boldsymbol\beta}\sum_j K_h(c_j-c_k) \left[q_j^{(r)}- \left(\beta_1+\beta_2t_{jk}+\beta_3t_{jk}^2\right)\right]^2, \qquad K_h(t)=h^{-1}\phi(t/h). \]

The term \(\beta_3t_{jk}^2\) allows local curvature. At the target, \(t=0\), so the fitted density height is the intercept \(\widehat\beta_1^{(r)}(c_k)\). Multiplying by the target-cell length gives its updated mass:

\[ \widetilde p_k^{(r+1)}=v_k\widehat\beta_1^{(r)}(c_k),\qquad \widehat p_k^{(r+1)} =\frac{[\widetilde p_k^{(r+1)}]_+} {\sum_m[\widetilde p_m^{(r+1)}]_+}, \qquad [a]_+=\max(a,0). \]

Thus the quadratic smooths the conditional density heights; the EM step still uses constant densities within cells. The regression has no extra cell-length weight. Singular fits fall back to a weighted constant, and zero-probability intervals trigger a \(10^{-8}\) mixture with uniform cell probabilities. Iteration stops when \(\max_j|\widehat p_j^{(r+1)}-\widehat p_j^{(r)}|<10^{-6}\), or warns at 200 iterations.

Convergence does not establish local-likelihood maximization.

Returned density. From the terminal masses \(\widehat{\mathbf p}\), the evaluator takes a fresh EM step and uses the form of equation (6):

\[ \widehat f_{\mathrm{out}}(x) =\sum_j\frac{[\mathcal M(\widehat{\mathbf p})]_j}{v_j} \int_{Q_j}K_h(u-x)\,du. \]

This is ordinary Gaussian reconstruction, not the fitted quadratic intercept. Its masses come from the modified iteration, so the result is not the paper’s original estimator. The curve integrates to one over the real line; its cumulative trapezoidal integral on the plotting grid is normalized to obtain \(\widehat F_j\).

Let \(f_Y\) be the true joint density of \((Y_1,Y_2)\), and \(F_j,f_j\) its true marginal CDFs and densities. With \(\Phi,\phi\) denoting the standard-normal CDF and density,

\[ Z_1=\Phi^{-1}\{F_1(Y_1)\},\qquad Z_2=\Phi^{-1}\{F_2(Y_2)\}. \]

The evaluation coordinates \(z_1,z_2\in\mathbb R\) are scalars, and \(f_Z(z_1,z_2)\) is a bivariate density. Each transformed observation has two coordinates; a sample is an \(n\times2\) matrix. Standard-normal margins do not imply a bivariate-normal joint distribution.

Fitting. Replace \(F_j\) by the estimated marginal CDF \(\widehat F_j\) and transform each censoring interval to

\[ \left[\Phi^{-1}\{\widehat F_j(L_{ij})\},\; \Phi^{-1}\{\widehat F_j(R_{ij})\}\right],\qquad j=1,2. \]

CDF values are clipped to \([10^{-6},1-10^{-6}]\) and transformed endpoints rounded outward with spacing \(0.2\). Denote the resulting endpoints by \(Z_{ij}^L,Z_{ij}^R\). EMS fits the rectangles \(R_i=[Z_{i1}^L,Z_{i1}^R]\times[Z_{i2}^L,Z_{i2}^R]\) using the same updates as above, with cell areas \(v_j=\lVert Q_j\rVert\). For source centre \((c_{j1},c_{j2})\) and target \((c_{k1},c_{k2})\), set \(t_1=(c_{j1}-c_{k1})/h_1\) and \(t_2=(c_{j2}-c_{k2})/h_2\). The full quadratic is

\[ \mathcal P(t_1,t_2;\boldsymbol\beta) =\beta_1+\beta_2t_1+\beta_3t_2 +\beta_4t_1^2+\beta_5t_1t_2+\beta_6t_2^2. \]

The product Gaussian kernel does not impose independence. The returned density uses the bivariate kernel reconstruction. list(x = zg, y = zg) specifies evaluation locations; censoring endpoints determine the fitting partition.

True reference. At \((z_1,z_2)\), the corresponding original-scale coordinates are

\[ y_1=F_1^{-1}\{\Phi(z_1)\},\qquad y_2=F_2^{-1}\{\Phi(z_2)\}. \]

Differentiating \(F_j(y_j)=\Phi(z_j)\) gives \(dy_j/dz_j=\phi(z_j)/f_j(y_j)\), so

\[ f_Z(z_1,z_2) =f_Y\left(F_1^{-1}\{\Phi(z_1)\},\;F_2^{-1}\{\Phi(z_2)\}\right) \frac{\phi(z_1)\phi(z_2)} {f_1\left(F_1^{-1}\{\Phi(z_1)\}\right)\, f_2\left(F_2^{-1}\{\Phi(z_2)\}\right)}. \]

The code uses the known SUN densities, numerical integration, and inverse-CDF interpolation to approximate this reference.

Copula. For \(u_1,u_2\in(0,1)\),

\[ c(u_1,u_2)=\frac{f_Z\bigl(\Phi^{-1}(u_1),\Phi^{-1}(u_2)\bigr)}{\phi\bigl(\Phi^{-1}(u_1)\bigr)\,\phi\bigl(\Phi^{-1}(u_2)\bigr)}. \]

Replacing \(f_Z\) by its estimate gives the estimated copula density, though this division does not enforce exactly uniform margins.

Joint density reconstruction

The estimated joint density on the original scale is reconstructed from \(\widehat f_Z(z_1,z_2)\) and the two EMS marginals. With \(z_j=\Phi^{-1}\{\widehat F_j(y_j)\}\) for \(j=1,2\),

\[ \widehat f_Y(y_1,y_2)=\widehat f_Z(z_1,z_2)\, \frac{\widehat f_1(y_1)\widehat f_2(y_2)}{\phi(z_1)\phi(z_2)}. \]

Marginal densities and CDFs share the same grid normalization; \(\widehat f_Z\) is evaluated by bilinear interpolation. Marginal transformations remain fixed during bivariate CV rather than being refitted within folds.

# Normalize the marginal curves consistently with the CDFs used by qnorm.
marginal_on_scale <- lapply(ems_marginals, function(fit) {
    grid <- fit$x
    dens <- pmax(as.numeric(fit$density), 0)
    total <- sum(diff(grid) * (head(dens, -1) + tail(dens, -1)) / 2)
    if (!is.finite(total) || total <= 0) {
        total <- 1
    }
    list(
        cdf = ems_monotone_cdf(fit),
        dens = stats::approxfun(grid, dens / total, rule = 2)
    )
})

Local-constant EMS comparison

The preceding analyses use a local-quadratic density smoother with Gaussian weights. Here, local-constant EMS fits the same samples under heavy and light censoring, with its own marginal and product-Gaussian bandwidth searches. Marginal searches retain the coarse grid and local refinement above. Bivariate searches use c(0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8) on each axis without refinement. Both use ten-fold CV. The new CV scores integrate the reconstructed density over the held-out intervals or rectangles.

The product-Gaussian local-constant fit integrates the kernel uniformly over each source cell, as described in R/ems_local_constant.R. Its smoothing transfers, density evaluations, and rectangle probabilities use the same fitted mixture. Marginal CDFs and density transformations below use the full fitted distributions directly. The normal-to-uniform transformation is retained for comparison; it does not impose uniform copula marginals.

# Keep every local-constant result inside its own list, separate from quadratic fits.
fit_local_constant_case <- function(margin_data, joint_data, grids, z_grid,
                                    u_grid, original_grid) {
    h0 <- vapply(seq_len(2), function(j) {
        ems_bandwidth((margin_data$left[, j] + margin_data$right[, j]) / 2)
    }, numeric(1))
    marginal_cv <- lapply(seq_len(2), function(j) {
        cv_ems_bandwidth(
            L = margin_data$left[, j], R = margin_data$right[, j],
            grid = grids[[j]], h0 = h0[j],
            multipliers = c(0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 8),
            refine = TRUE, refine_points = 10, refine_width = 1,
            n_folds = 10, degree = 0, kernel = "gaussian", maxit = 200, tol = 1e-6,
            seed = 20260626
        )
    })
    coarse_best <- lapply(marginal_cv, function(cv) {
        scores <- cv$coarse$summary
        scores[which.max(scores$total_holdout_loglik), , drop = FALSE]
    })
    fit_margin <- function(j, h) {
        ems_density_constant(
            L = margin_data$left[, j], R = margin_data$right[, j],
            h = h, grid = grids[[j]], degree = 0, kernel = "gaussian", maxit = 200, tol = 1e-6
        )
    }
    marginals <- lapply(seq_len(2), function(j) fit_margin(j, marginal_cv[[j]]$best$h))
    coarse_marginals <- lapply(seq_len(2), function(j) fit_margin(j, coarse_best[[j]]$h))

    # The helpers use the analytic CDF for local-constant marginal fits.
    limits <- pseudo_normal_limits_from_ems(
        joint_data$left, joint_data$right, marginals, round = 0.2
    )
    z_midpoints <- ems_pseudo_normal(
        (joint_data$left + joint_data$right) / 2, marginals
    )
    base <- gaussian_bandwidth_base(z_midpoints)
    joint_cv <- cv_ems_product_bandwidth(
        Lx = limits$lower[, 1], Rx = limits$upper[, 1],
        Ly = limits$lower[, 2], Ry = limits$upper[, 2],
        h0 = base$h,
        multipliers = c(0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8),
        refine = FALSE,
        exclude = rbind(c(1, 8), c(8, 1)),
        n_folds = 10, degree = 0, kernel = "gaussian", maxit = 200, tol = 1e-6,
        seed = 20260626, n_threads = 2
    )
    joint_fit <- ems_density_constant(
        L = limits$lower[, 1], R = limits$upper[, 1],
        Ly = limits$lower[, 2], Ry = limits$upper[, 2],
        h = c(joint_cv$best$h1, joint_cv$best$h2),
        degree = 0, kernel = "gaussian",
        grid = list(x = z_grid, y = z_grid),
        maxit = 200, tol = 1e-6, n_threads = 2
    )

    # Evaluate the fitted mixture directly, including queries outside the display grid.
    uu <- expand.grid(u1 = u_grid, u2 = u_grid)
    zu <- cbind(stats::qnorm(uu$u1), stats::qnorm(uu$u2))
    copula_density <- matrix(
        ems_constant_density(joint_fit, zu) /
            (stats::dnorm(zu[, 1]) * stats::dnorm(zu[, 2])),
        nrow = length(u_grid), ncol = length(u_grid)
    )
    yy <- expand.grid(y1 = original_grid$x, y2 = original_grid$y)
    z1 <- stats::qnorm(clamp_probability(ems_constant_cdf(marginals[[1]], yy$y1)))
    z2 <- stats::qnorm(clamp_probability(ems_constant_cdf(marginals[[2]], yy$y2)))
    rebuilt <- ems_constant_density(joint_fit, cbind(z1, z2)) *
        ems_constant_density(marginals[[1]], yy$y1) *
        ems_constant_density(marginals[[2]], yy$y2) /
        (stats::dnorm(z1) * stats::dnorm(z2))

    list(
        marginal_cv = marginal_cv, marginals = marginals,
        coarse_best = coarse_best, coarse_marginals = coarse_marginals,
        limits = limits, base = base, joint_cv = joint_cv, joint_fit = joint_fit,
        z_grid = z_grid, u_grid = u_grid, copula_density = copula_density,
        original_grid = original_grid,
        rebuilt_density = matrix(rebuilt, nrow = length(original_grid$x))
    )
}

# Report the selected multiplier and actual bandwidth separately.
local_constant_bandwidth_table <- function(result) {
    marginal_rows <- lapply(seq_len(2), function(j) {
        best <- result$marginal_cv[[j]]$best
        data.frame(
            Component = paste("Margin", j),
            Coarse = as.character(signif(result$coarse_best[[j]]$multiplier, 4)),
            Final = as.character(signif(best$multiplier, 4)),
            Bandwidth = as.character(signif(best$h, 4)),
            CV_loglik = best$total_holdout_loglik,
            Converged = result$marginals[[j]]$converged,
            Iterations = result$marginals[[j]]$iterations
        )
    })
    scores <- result$joint_cv$coarse$summary
    coarse <- scores[which.max(scores$total_holdout_loglik), ]
    best <- result$joint_cv$best
    pair <- function(a, b) paste(signif(c(a, b), 4), collapse = ", ")
    rbind(do.call(rbind, marginal_rows), data.frame(
        Component = "Normal coordinates 1, 2",
        Coarse = pair(coarse$multiplier_1, coarse$multiplier_2),
        Final = pair(best$multiplier_1, best$multiplier_2),
        Bandwidth = pair(best$h1, best$h2), CV_loglik = best$total_holdout_loglik,
        Converged = result$joint_fit$converged, Iterations = result$joint_fit$iterations
    ))
}

Local log-quadratic estimation following Fan et al. (2011)

This section follows the same order as the preceding analyses: estimate the two marginals, transform the censoring intervals to normal scores, estimate dependence there, and reconstruct the joint density. Each density fit uses a local log-quadratic extension of the local-likelihood framework in Fan, Stafford and Brown (2011). Gaussian kernels give an analytic local-likelihood solution. The marginal fits are separate one-dimensional models; they are not obtained by integrating a bivariate fit.

Heavy, intermediate, and light censoring each use all 4,000 observations. As above, the heavy marginal intervals have rounding step \(0.02\), and its dependence intervals have rounding step \(0.2\). The intermediate case uses one-half exponential half-widths and rounding step \(0.1\) for both stages. The light case uses one-quarter exponential half-widths and rounding step \(0.05\) for both stages. The interval draws are paired across all three censoring cases using the same seed. The intermediate case is included only in these Fan comparisons. True SUN parameters and latent observations are used for simulation and reference densities, not for fitting or selecting bandwidths.

1. Estimate the marginal densities

For each margin \(j\), Fan’s local model is

\[ \lambda_j(u;\mathbf a,s) =\exp\{a_1+a_2(u-s)+a_3(u-s)^2\}. \]

Given current cell probabilities \(\Lambda_\ell^{(r)}\), the conditional allocation is

\[ M_\ell^{(r)}=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{\Lambda_\ell^{(r)}\mathcal I_{i\ell}} {\sum_m\Lambda_m^{(r)}\mathcal I_{im}},\qquad q^{(r)}(u)=\frac{M_\ell^{(r)}}{|Q_\ell|}\quad(u\in Q_\ell). \]

This is the EM allocation in Braun and Stafford’s equation (5). Fan et al.’s density-estimation expected local likelihood, equation (2.9), then fits the log-quadratic polynomial \(\mathcal P\) by maximizing

\[ \widetilde\ell_s(\mathbf a) =n\int K_h(u-s)q^{(r)}(u)\mathcal P(u-s;\mathbf a)\,du -n\int K_h(u-s)\exp\{\mathcal P(u-s;\mathbf a)\}\,du. \]

With a Gaussian kernel, let \(t=(u-s)/h\) and define the local kernel mass \(s_0=\int K_h(u-s)q^{(r)}(u)\,du\), weighted mean \(\mu\), and weighted variance \(v\) of \(t\). Matching these moments solves the three score equations and gives

\[ f_{j,\mathrm{raw}}(s)=\exp\{\widehat a_1(s)\} =\frac{s_0}{\sqrt v}\exp\left(-\frac{\mu^2}{2v}\right). \]

The final smooth marginal density is \(\widehat f_j=f_{j,\mathrm{raw}}/\int_{D_j}f_{j,\mathrm{raw}}\), normalized over its padded computational interval \(D_j\). Its CDF \(\widehat F_j\) is computed by integrating that same smooth density. Cells provide the numerical conditional-allocation approximation; the plotted density evaluates the fitted local intercept directly.

Each marginal bandwidth uses ten-fold CV. The coarse multipliers are \(0.5,1,2,3,4,5,8\); ten equally spaced candidates within one unit of the coarse winner form the refinement stage, with a lower bound of \(0.5\). The final winner is selected across both stages using identical folds. The marginal figures show the final fit in red, the best coarse fit in dashed blue, and the analytical SUN marginal in black.

2. Transform the intervals and estimate dependence

The separately fitted marginal CDFs map each observed rectangle to

\[ Z_{ij}^L=\Phi^{-1}\{\widehat F_j(L_{ij})\},\qquad Z_{ij}^R=\Phi^{-1}\{\widehat F_j(R_{ij})\}. \]

Numerically, the probabilities are limited to \([10^{-6},1-10^{-6}]\) before applying \(\Phi^{-1}\), and the transformed endpoints are rounded outward with spacing \(0.2\). The marginal transformations remain fixed during dependence bandwidth CV. The bivariate fit therefore uses only the transformed censoring rectangles; its true-density reference uses the true SUN marginal map, as in the preceding sections.

Fan’s bivariate local model on this normal-score scale uses the full polynomial

\[ \mathcal P(t;\mathbf a) =a_1+a_2t_1+a_3t_2+a_4t_1^2+a_5t_1t_2+a_6t_2^2. \]

The same conditional allocation and expected local likelihood apply in two dimensions. For standardized Gaussian-kernel coordinates \(t=H^{-1}(u-s)\), let \(s_0\) be the conditional kernel mass and let \(\mu,V\) be its weighted mean vector and covariance matrix. The fitted intercept is

\[ g_{\mathrm{raw}}(s)=\exp\{\widehat a_1(s)\} =\frac{s_0}{\sqrt{\det V}}\exp\left(-\frac12\mu^\top V^{-1}\mu\right). \]

Equivalently, Fan’s intercept score divides the conditional-kernel numerator by \(\Psi(s;\widehat{\mathbf a}_{-1})=n\int K_h(u-s)\exp\{\mathcal P(u-s;\widehat{\mathbf a}_{-1})\}\,du\), with the intercept replaced by zero in \(\mathbf a_{-1}\). This is the log-quadratic counterpart of Braun and Stafford’s equation (6).

The bivariate density \(\widehat g\) is the smooth intercept normalized once over its padded normal-score domain. Source-cell Gaussian moments include the interaction term, and target-cell integration supplies the next EM probabilities. The plotted normal-score density evaluates the smooth intercept directly. Gaussian product-kernel bandwidths have one pilot scale per normal coordinate, estimated from transformed observation midpoints. Five-fold CV searches the common multipliers \(1,4,8,10,12\) applied to both pilot scales, with no bivariate bandwidth refinement. Held-out rectangle probabilities use the numerical fitted-cell approximation. CV scores compare candidates within the same censoring case; they do not compare different censoring severities.

3. Form the copula and reconstruct the joint density

The fitted normal-score density gives

\[ \widehat c(u_1,u_2) =\frac{\widehat g\{\Phi^{-1}(u_1),\Phi^{-1}(u_2)\}} {\phi\{\Phi^{-1}(u_1)\}\phi\{\Phi^{-1}(u_2)\}}, \]

and the original-scale joint density is reconstructed as

\[ \widehat f_Y(y_1,y_2) =\widehat c\{\widehat F_1(y_1),\widehat F_2(y_2)\} \widehat f_1(y_1)\widehat f_2(y_2). \]

This uses the one-dimensional Fan marginals fitted in the first stage. The normal-score transformation alone does not force \(\widehat g\) to have exactly standard-normal margins, so the transformed \(\widehat c\) need not have exactly uniform margins. Consequently, the reconstructed joint density need not reproduce the supplied marginal curves exactly. No marginal balancing is applied.
